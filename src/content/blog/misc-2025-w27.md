# Building Smarter Workflows: Lenskart, Kissflow, and Action Hub Improvements

## Overview

This past week, our engineering efforts were sharply focused on enhancing the operational efficiency and communication capabilities across our platform, with a significant emphasis on the Lenskart workflow. The core objective was to move beyond manual processes, leveraging deeper integrations and structured data management to provide more accurate insights and streamline critical business operations.

A major thrust involved fortifying our integration with Kissflow, an external workflow management system, to achieve real-time synchronization of property status updates. This was complemented by the development of intelligent automation to identify and reject Lenskart properties that failed to meet specific physical criteria post-survey. Concurrently, we dedicated substantial resources to improving internal tooling, specifically by creating a robust reporting mechanism for user activity and migrating critical video fields within our survey forms to ensure data integrity and future compatibility. Perhaps most fundamentally, our core chat system underwent a significant refactoring, transitioning from a more monolithic structure to one that supports distinct, entity-specific conversations, laying the groundwork for more sophisticated in-app communication. These initiatives collectively aim to reduce manual overhead, improve data reliability, and foster a more collaborative and data-driven environment for our users and internal teams.

## Problem

Our existing systems, while functional, presented several bottlenecks and areas for improvement that hindered efficiency, data integrity, and scalability.

Firstly, the **lack of automated integration for property status updates from external systems like Kissflow** was a significant operational pain point. Once a property moved into Kissflow for further processing, its status in our internal database often lagged behind, requiring manual checks, data entry, or delayed synchronization. This manual intervention was not only time-consuming but also prone to human error, leading to outdated information for our teams and impacting the speed of decision-making. The absence of real-time feedback loops created a disconnect between our platform and external workflows, causing friction in the property lifecycle management.

Secondly, we faced an **inefficient identification and rejection process for Lenskart properties that did not meet specific physical criteria** after survey completion. Lenskart, a key client, has stringent requirements for store size (e.g., less than 400 sqft) and signage height (e.g., less than 14 ft). Previously, identifying properties that failed these criteria involved manual review of survey data, a laborious and error-prone task, especially with a growing volume of submissions. Properties that didn't meet these standards would progress further in the pipeline than they should, wasting resources and delaying the final selection process.

Thirdly, there was a glaring **absence of a structured, user-friendly reporting mechanism to track user activity** for specific accounts. Business Development and Account Management teams needed clear insights into key metrics such as properties added, surveys completed, and presentations created by their users. Without an automated report, gathering this data was a manual aggregation effort, making it difficult to assess team productivity, identify high-performing users, or pinpoint areas needing support. This lack of visibility hampered data-driven decision-making for account management.

Fourth, we encountered **inconsistent or outdated schema for video fields within survey forms (`aps_survey` JSON)**. Over time, the way video data was captured and stored had evolved, leading to a fragmented schema. Some fields might have expected a single video picker, while newer requirements demanded multi-video pickers or specific fields for video names and links. This inconsistency led to potential data integrity issues, difficulties in rendering survey forms correctly, and challenges in consuming video data reliably across different parts of the application or for analytics. It created a technical debt that needed immediate attention to ensure robust data handling.

Fifth, our existing **chat system was monolithic and lacked flexibility**, failing to clearly distinguish between different chat entities. Whether it was an internal discussion about a property, a direct chat with an agent, or a conversation specific to a report, the system treated them all similarly. This monolithic design hindered scalability, made it difficult to implement entity-specific features (like notifications for property updates within a property chat), and complicated future development efforts. The lack of clear separation made the chat experience less intuitive and less powerful for users needing context-specific communication.

Finally, a crucial piece of data, the **"Submission ID" (`aps_id`), was missing in data payloads sent to Zvolv**, another external system. This omission created a significant traceability gap, making it challenging to link data points between our platform and Zvolv, hindering end-to-end process visibility and data reconciliation. Without a unique identifier like `aps_id`, correlating information across systems became a manual, error-prone task, impacting the integrity of integrated workflows.

## Solution

Addressing these challenges required a multi-pronged approach, encompassing API development, scripting for automation, schema migration, and significant refactoring of core modules.

To tackle the manual synchronization of property statuses, we **implemented a new API endpoint (`getPropertyStatusFromKissflow`) and associated helper functions** within our Flask application. This endpoint was designed to act as a webhook receiver, allowing Kissflow to push real-time status updates to our system. Upon receiving a payload, our backend processes the data, validates it against predefined mappings (e.g., `webhook_property_status_string_to_int` in `gra_constants.py` as seen in Commit 771640d11fb06cb2db4ce621a35906d7fedfe952), and updates the corresponding property records in our PostgreSQL database. This automation, registered in `rest_api.py` (Commit 771640d11fb06cb2db4ce621a35906d7fedfe952), ensures our internal data reflects the most current status from Kissflow, significantly reducing manual effort and improving data accuracy. Furthermore, this integration now triggers automated Slack notifications for relevant stakeholders, ensuring immediate awareness of status changes.

For the inefficient identification and rejection of Lenskart properties, we **developed a targeted script (`get_lk_properties_to_reject`)** within `sheet_import_scripts.py` (Commit e9cc3bda296bc0ad03f0de4fae5b42ea5153614b). This Python script leverages Pandas to process completed survey data, specifically checking for Lenskart properties where the store size is less than 400 sqft or signage height is less than 14 ft. The script can automatically flag these properties for rejection, and optionally, export a CSV file for review and analysis. This automation drastically reduces the manual effort involved in quality control for Lenskart properties, ensuring only compliant properties proceed in the pipeline and saving valuable operational resources.

To provide better insights into user activity, we **created a comprehensive reporting script (`get_bd_user_wise_breakdown_of_properties_and_surveys`)**. This function, added as part of Commit 42610d34c560ecc3a9edc4c9f1f1ef636e8c4d69, generates a user-wise breakdown of key activities - properties added, surveys completed, and presentations created - for a given account ID. The generated data is then exported to both local CSV files and Google Cloud Storage (GCS) via `gcsfs`, making it easily accessible for business development and account management teams. This provides a structured, automated mechanism for tracking team productivity and engagement, enabling data-driven decisions.

Addressing the inconsistent video field schema required a dedicated migration effort. We **introduced `VideoMigration` and `VideoFieldUpdater` scripts** in `sheet_import_scripts.py` (Commit 771640d11fb06cb2db4ce621a35906d7fedfe952). These scripts are designed to transform and update the `aps_survey` JSON schema, ensuring consistency for video fields across all existing survey forms. Specifically, they handle scenarios like converting single video pickers to multi-video pickers and adding new, standardized fields for video names and links. This proactive migration ensures data integrity, prevents display issues in the UI, and standardizes how video content is managed within our survey system, laying a robust foundation for future multimedia features.

The monolithic chat system underwent a substantial overhaul. We **refactored the chat module to introduce distinct `internal_chat` (for properties/reports) and `agent_chat` entities**. This involved creating new database tables like `gra_internal_chats`, `gra_agent_chats`, and `gra_action_hub_chat`, and implementing specific `get_or_create` methods for these entities in `chat_module.py` (Commit c0f765a4731192e84896742559975ce8d9f06a6d). Furthermore, dedicated message creation functions (`create_internal_message`, `create_agent_message`, `create_report_message`) were developed to ensure messages are correctly associated with their respective chat contexts. A new file, `v3/modules/action_hub/chat_new.py`, containing `class ChatHelper(DBUtil):` (Commit c0f765a4731192e84896742559975ce8d9f06a6d), encapsulates this new, more modular chat logic. This refactoring significantly enhances the scalability, maintainability, and feature development potential of our in-app communication, allowing for more structured and context-aware conversations.

Finally, to improve data traceability with Zvolv, we **added the "Submission ID" (`aps_id`) to the Zvolv data payload** in `bd_common_helper.py`. This was achieved by including `{\"label\": \"Submission ID\", \"value\": form_info.get(\"aps_id\")}` (Commit ebcfab27bbfb302623b4aa080fbce756ebc0ef65). This seemingly small addition closes a critical data gap, ensuring that every data point sent to Zvolv can be uniquely identified and linked back to its origin in our system, thereby improving data integrity and reconciliation across integrated workflows.

Beyond these core solutions, other related efforts included adding `generate_kissflow_store_codes` to `sheet_import_scripts.py` (Commit 4833c83807e976fa6b4942ac4190e38cf48863ec) to facilitate Lenskart broker onboarding via WhatsApp and updating property statuses in custom markets, further solidifying the Lenskart workflow.

## Tradeoffs

While the solutions implemented this week brought significant advancements, they also involved certain tradeoffs and considerations that are important to acknowledge for a holistic understanding of our engineering decisions.

One notable decision was the **commenting out of `handle_exceptions` in `bd_helper.py`** (Commit 4833c83807e976fa6b4942ac4190e38cf48863ec). This was a deliberate, albeit temporary, measure taken to facilitate the transition to a new V2 exception handling flow. The immediate implication is a potential reduction in immediate error visibility or an alteration of the standard exception flow for certain legacy components. While this might introduce a temporary period where error logging or handling deviates from the established pattern, the long-term benefit is a more standardized, robust, and potentially more granular exception management system in the V2 architecture. This allows for better categorization, reporting, and response to errors, ultimately improving system stability and maintainability. The tradeoff here is a short-term adjustment in error handling behavior for a long-term architectural improvement.

A significant portion of the work, particularly the Kissflow integration, Lenskart property rejection, and Lenskart broker onboarding, is **highly tailored to the "Lenskart" account (`ai_id = 442`)**. While this specificity ensures maximum impact and efficiency for a critical client, it inherently limits the immediate reusability of these components for other clients without further abstraction or modification. The tradeoff is between delivering highly optimized, client-specific solutions quickly versus investing more time upfront in building generalized, configurable modules. For strategic clients like Lenskart, the former often takes precedence to meet immediate business needs, with the understanding that generalization might be a future iteration if similar requirements emerge from other clients.

Several solutions, such as `update_ap_property_status`, `VideoMigration`, and `get_lk_properties_to_reject`, are **implemented as standalone scripts**. While this approach allows for rapid development and deployment of specific functionalities, it also means these scripts might require manual execution or separate scheduling mechanisms (e.g., cron jobs) rather than being fully integrated into the application's real-time, event-driven flows. The tradeoff here is between development speed and operational overhead. Standalone scripts are quicker to build and test for specific tasks, but they add to the complexity of deployment and monitoring compared to features seamlessly integrated into the core application logic. This decision often balances immediate need with available resources and the criticality of real-time processing for the given task.

Finally, the introduction of new database tables like **`gra_internal_chats`, `gra_agent_chats`, and `gra_action_hub_chat` (along with `gra_property_webhook_status`) adds more tables and relationships to the database schema**. While this is essential for the refactored chat system to achieve its desired modularity and context-specificity, it inherently increases the complexity of the database schema. More tables can lead to more complex joins for comprehensive data retrieval, potentially impacting query performance if not carefully optimized. The tradeoff is between a simpler, less normalized schema (which would have limited chat functionality) and a more normalized, feature-rich schema that requires more thoughtful database management and query optimization. This is a necessary evolution to support advanced features, but it demands ongoing attention to database performance and design.

## Notable Tech

The engineering work this week leveraged a robust and familiar set of technologies, primarily within the Python ecosystem, to deliver the various solutions.

*   **Python**: As the primary language for our backend services and scripting, Python was instrumental across all facets of development. Its versatility and rich ecosystem enabled rapid development of API endpoints, data processing scripts, and complex logic for workflow automation.
*   **PostgreSQL**: Our relational database of choice, PostgreSQL served as the persistent data store for all application data, including property statuses, survey responses, and the newly structured chat entities. Its reliability and advanced features were crucial for managing complex data relationships and ensuring data integrity.
*   **Flask/REST API**: The Flask microframework was used to build and expose our RESTful API endpoints, most notably the `getPropertyStatusFromKissflow` endpoint. Flask's lightweight nature allowed for efficient development of web services that could seamlessly integrate with external systems like Kissflow.
*   **Pandas**: This powerful Python library was extensively used for data manipulation and analysis, particularly in scripts like `get_lk_properties_to_reject` and `get_bd_user_wise_breakdown_of_properties_and_surveys`. Pandas' DataFrames provided an efficient way to process, filter, and transform large datasets, enabling quick insights and automated decision-making.
*   **GCSFS (Google Cloud Storage File System)**: Utilized for interacting with Google Cloud Storage, GCSFS allowed our reporting scripts to seamlessly export generated CSV files to the cloud. This facilitated easy access and sharing of reports, ensuring data availability for stakeholders.
*   **JSON Parsing/Manipulation**: Given the nature of survey data (`aps_survey` JSON) and API payloads (from Kissflow), extensive JSON parsing and manipulation were performed. This was critical for schema migrations, data extraction, and ensuring compatibility between systems.
*   **Slack Integration**: Our internal communication platform, Slack, was integrated to provide real-time notifications for critical events, such as property status updates from Kissflow. This ensures that relevant teams are immediately informed, improving operational awareness and response times.
*   **WhatsApp API (implied)**: While not directly implemented this week, the context of Lenskart broker onboarding via WhatsApp implies interaction with or preparation for using the WhatsApp API for automated communication, showcasing our platform's ability to integrate with diverse communication channels.
*   **SQLAlchemy**: As our Object-Relational Mapper (ORM), SQLAlchemy provided an abstraction layer over PostgreSQL, allowing us to interact with the database using Python objects rather than raw SQL. This improved code readability, maintainability, and reduced the risk of SQL injection vulnerabilities, especially with the introduction of new tables for chat entities.
*   **Socket.IO (implied by `SocketHandler`)**: Although not explicitly detailed in the solutions, the mention of `SocketHandler` in the broader context of chat systems implies the use of Socket.IO for real-time, bidirectional communication between clients and the server. This technology is fundamental for delivering a responsive and interactive chat experience.

This blend of technologies allowed us to build robust, scalable, and efficient solutions that addressed complex business requirements while maintaining a cohesive and manageable tech stack.

## Outcomes

The engineering efforts of this past week have yielded significant positive outcomes, fundamentally improving our operational workflows, data integrity, and communication capabilities.

**Enhanced Operational Efficiency and Automation:** The most immediate impact is the drastic reduction in manual effort across several key processes. The real-time Kissflow integration for property status updates has virtually eliminated the need for manual synchronization, ensuring our internal database is always up-to-date. This translates to faster decision-making, reduced administrative overhead, and a more streamlined property lifecycle management. Similarly, the automated rejection script for Lenskart properties has significantly improved quality control, preventing non-compliant properties from progressing further and saving valuable resources that would have been wasted on manual reviews. This automation ensures higher data quality at the source for a critical client.

**Improved Data Integrity and Reliability:** The video field migration scripts have successfully standardized the `aps_survey` JSON schema, resolving inconsistencies and preventing potential data loss or display issues. This ensures that video content, a crucial component of our survey data, is captured and stored reliably, laying a solid foundation for future multimedia features. The addition of the "Submission ID" (`aps_id`) to the Zvolv data payload has closed a critical traceability gap, enabling seamless data linking and reconciliation between our platform and Zvolv, thereby improving the integrity of our integrated workflows.

**Better Insights and Accountability:** The new user-wise breakdown report provides invaluable analytics for Business Development and Account Management teams. By automatically generating reports on properties added, surveys completed, and presentations created, managers now have clear, data-driven insights into individual and team performance. This fosters greater accountability, helps identify high-performing users, and pinpoints areas where additional support or training might be needed, ultimately driving better strategic decisions.

**Scalable and Context-Aware Communication:** The comprehensive refactoring of the chat module represents a foundational improvement to our in-app communication. By introducing distinct `internal_chat` and `agent_chat` entities, along with specific message creation functions, the system is now far more scalable, maintainable, and capable of supporting richer, context-specific conversations. This lays the groundwork for advanced collaboration features, allowing users to engage in discussions directly relevant to specific properties, reports, or agents, thereby enhancing overall user experience and team collaboration. The new modular design also makes future feature development for chat significantly easier and less prone to introducing regressions.

**Stronger Client Relationships:** The Lenskart-specific automations, including the Kissflow integration and the property rejection logic, directly address critical client requirements. By automating these processes, we demonstrate our commitment to supporting Lenskart's unique operational needs, leading to a more efficient partnership and strengthening our client relationships. The ability to quickly onboard brokers via WhatsApp and update custom market statuses further solidifies this tailored support.

In summary, this week's engineering endeavors have not only resolved immediate pain points but have also strategically positioned our platform for future growth and enhanced capabilities. We've moved closer to a fully automated, data-driven ecosystem, where efficiency, data integrity, and intelligent communication are at the forefront of our operations.
